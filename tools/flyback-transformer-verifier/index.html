Measurement guide
Step-by-step LCR meter instructions, wiring diagrams, dot notation, and what to expect — for anyone without prior experience
Understanding dot notation
●
What the dot (•) means on a transformer
This is the most important concept before measuring anything
Flyback transformer — dot notation
1
What the dot means
The dot (•) marks the start of each winding — the terminal where current enters when the winding is energised in the positive direction. All dotted terminals go positive at the same moment in the AC cycle.
2
Why it matters for flyback
In a flyback, the secondary is inverted relative to a forward converter — energy is stored then released. The dot tells you the polarity so you wire the diode the right way. If the dot is on S1, the diode anode goes to S1.
3
How to verify the dot on a received sample
Connect a 9 V battery (+) to pin P1 (dotted primary pin) through a 1 kΩ resistor. The moment you connect, momentarily touch then remove. Watch the secondary with a voltmeter or scope. The dotted secondary pin (S1) will go positive. If it goes negative, the dot is reversed or winding is backwards.
Tip: Do NOT hold the battery connected — the transformer is not rated for DC steady-state. A 50–100 ms tap is enough to see the polarity pulse.
4
Reading the schematic symbols
On pin-out diagrams: ● = dotted start pin. On winding diagrams, the spiral that starts at the dot wraps in the same direction on both primary and secondary. Opposite dot positions = opposite polarity (used in push-pull or for negative rails).

Electrical measurements — LCR meter setup
M1
Primary inductance — Lp
The most important parameter. Determines peak current, Bmax and stored energy.
LCR Hi → P1(dot) · Lo → P2 · Secondary: OPEN
1
Leave all secondary and auxiliary pins open (floating)
Do not connect anything to S1, S2, or auxiliary winding pins. Any load or short on secondary will couple back and corrupt the Lp reading.
2
Connect LCR meter: Hi → P1 (dotted pin), Lo → P2
Use Kelvin clips or a proper SMD tweezer fixture if available. For through-hole transformers, ensure firm contact on the pins — oxidized pins can add 5–15% error.
3
Set LCR meter: L–Q or L–D mode · 100 kHz · 0.1 Vrms · no DC bias
The standard test frequency is 100 kHz unless the datasheet specifies otherwise. Use the lowest signal level available (0.1 V) to stay in the linear magnetic region. Series mode (Ls) is preferred for high-Q inductors; parallel mode (Lp) for lossy ones — choose series for transformer primaries.
If your meter shows only one frequency, use 1 kHz and note it — values will differ but the ratio Llk/Lp stays consistent.
4
Record the Ls reading and the Q factor
Enter Ls as Lp in the sample inspection page. Q should typically be > 20 at 100 kHz for a well-designed flyback primary. Low Q (<10) may indicate a lossy core or incorrect frequency selection.

M2
Leakage inductance — Llk
Tells you how tightly the primary and secondary are coupled. Drives snubber design.
LCR Hi → P1 · Lo → P2 · Secondary: SHORT S1–S2
1
Short-circuit all secondary pins together
Connect S1 to S2 with a short piece of wire or a clip lead. If there is an auxiliary winding, short its pins too. Any un-shorted winding will prevent a clean leakage measurement.
2
Connect LCR meter exactly as for M1: Hi → P1, Lo → P2
Same connection as Lp measurement — only the secondary changes. Keep the same frequency (100 kHz) and mode (series L).
3
Read the inductance — this is Llk
The value you read now is the leakage inductance referred to the primary. It is typically 1–5% of Lp. If it reads close to Lp, the secondary short is not making good contact.
4
Calculate coupling coefficient k
k = √(1 − Llk/Lp). A well-designed transformer should have k > 0.97. Enter both values in the sample inspection page — the ratio is checked automatically.

Expected: Llk = 1–5% of Lp. If Llk/Lp > 8% coupling is poor.
⚠ If Llk reading is unstable or changes when you wiggle the short wire, the clip connection has resistance — use a firmer connection.
M3
DC resistance — Rdc (primary and secondary)
Indicates winding quality, wire gauge correctness and copper loss.
Ω Hi → P1 · Ω Lo → P2 · others open. Repeat for S1→S2.
1
Use DC resistance mode (Rdc or DCR) on your LCR meter, or a milliohm meter
Most LCR meters have a DCR or R-DC mode. Select it. If using a multimeter, choose the lowest ohm range (e.g. 200 Ω). Zero/null the leads first — clip them together and press NULL or ZERO. This cancels lead resistance (typically 50–200 mΩ).
⚠ Zeroing the leads is critical — without it your reading will be off by the lead resistance, which may be larger than the winding resistance itself.
2
Primary Rdc: Hi → P1, Lo → P2, all others open
Leave all secondary pins floating. Record the reading in mΩ. A typical primary Rdc for a 60 W flyback is 150–600 mΩ depending on wire gauge and turns count.
3
Secondary Rdc: move Hi → S1, Lo → S2, all others open
Disconnect primary leads. Connect to the secondary pins. Secondary Rdc is often much lower (10–50 mΩ for high-current secondaries). A 4-wire Kelvin measurement is strongly preferred here.
4
What the values tell you
High Rdc means thinner wire or more turns than specified. Low Rdc may mean fewer turns (verify with turns ratio test). Copper loss in primary = Ip_rms² × Rdc_p. A rough estimate: keep Rdc loss below 0.5% of rated power per winding.
M4
Turns ratio verification
Confirms Np/Ns ratio and winding polarity (dot direction) simultaneously.
Apply low Vac to P1–P2, read Vac at S1–S2
1
Apply a safe low AC voltage to the primary — 5 to 12 Vac
Use a signal generator, a small 9 V toroidal isolation transformer, or a Variac set to 10 V. Connect to P1 (dotted) and P2. Do NOT use mains voltage — you only need a few volts to verify the ratio safely. Keep all other pins free.
⚠ Use an isolation transformer between mains and your test setup. Never apply mains directly to transformer pins without proper isolation.
2
Measure Vac at S1–S2 with a multimeter in VAC mode
Calculate: Turns ratio n = Vin / Vsec. For example: Vin = 10.00 V, Vsec = 1.25 V → n = 8.0 which matches Np:Ns = 48:6. Compare to the specified ratio.
3
Check polarity to confirm dot position
Switch your meter to DC mode (or use a scope). With the (+) terminal of your AC source connected to P1 (dotted), the dotted secondary pin S1 should be positive. If it reads negative, the dot on the secondary is reversed from the schematic — note this for your report.
4
Repeat for auxiliary winding
Apply Vin to primary as before. Measure Vaux between Aux1 and Aux2. Calculate aux turns ratio. This confirms the auxiliary voltage at your chosen Np/Ns ratio.
Expected: ratio within ±3% of Np/Ns. Polarity matches dot marking on schematic.
M5
Hipot (dielectric withstand) & isolation resistance
Safety-critical — verifies insulation between primary and secondary.
Hi-pot: all primary pins tied → Hi. All secondary pins tied → Lo.
!
Safety first — high voltage warning
Hipot testing applies voltages of 1500–4000 Vac. This is lethal. Use a purpose-built hipot tester with auto-discharge. Do not touch the transformer or test leads during the test. Ensure the test area is clear of personnel.
1
Tie all primary pins together and connect to HV Hi terminal
P1 and P2 (and any other primary pins) are connected together with clip leads. This combined node connects to the Hi (high voltage) terminal of the hipot tester.
2
Tie all secondary and auxiliary pins together → Lo terminal
S1, S2, and all auxiliary pins tied together. Connect this combined node to the Lo (return) terminal. The test measures the insulation between the primary bundle and the secondary bundle.
3
Apply test voltage for 60 seconds and read leakage current
Set the hipot tester to the voltage specified in the datasheet (typically 1500–3000 Vac for reinforced insulation). Apply for 60 s. Leakage current must stay below the tester's trip level — typically 1–10 mA. If the tester trips, the insulation has failed.
4
Isolation resistance: same wiring, 500 Vdc, megohmmeter
After hipot, use a 500 Vdc megohmmeter with the same connection. Apply for 60 s. Result should be > 100 MΩ. Values below 10 MΩ indicate moisture or insulation damage. Record both time and temperature — insulation resistance is temperature-sensitive.
Hipot: no trip, leakage < 1 mA. Isolation resistance: ≥ 100 MΩ @ 500 Vdc.
